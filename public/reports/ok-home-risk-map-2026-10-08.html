Data: FEMA, USDA Forest Service, NOAA, NIFC, U.S. Census Bureau

Each of Oklahoma's 663 residential ZIP codes is scored for severe weather risk, wildfire risk and homeowner demographics. 32 ZIP codes meet every prime target test and hold 17.2% of the state's owner-occupied homes. 369 ZIP codes are High or Extreme on at least one risk layer and hold 41.3%. Both campaigns run statewide, with Pittsburg and McIntosh counties excluded since October 7.

    Recommendations

    1
    Keep statewide delivery through the first account review
    Both campaigns stay statewide, minus Pittsburg and McIntosh counties. At the first review we compare where leads come from against these risk tiers.
    2
    Start any ZIP exclusions with Extreme ZIP codes
    134 ZIP codes are Extreme on severe weather or wildfire and hold 6.5% of owner-occupied homes. High-only ZIP codes hold 34.8%, including most of Oklahoma City and Norman, and stay on review.
    3
    Review counties with heavy recent fire activity
    Osage, Pushmataha, Adair, Latimer, Hughes, Cherokee, Le Flore each had 11 or more wildfires of 100+ acres in the last 12 months. Any county or ZIP code flagged by carrier wildfire scores can be excluded from both campaigns the same day.
    4
    Use prime target ZIP codes for added focus on Home
    32 ZIP codes meet every prime target test, led by the Tulsa and Canadian County suburbs. Elgin (73538) and Marlow (73055) are the prime target ZIP codes in southwest Oklahoma.
    5
    Run the same research for Texas before Texas campaigns start
    The same severe weather, wildfire and homeowner scoring applies to Texas ZIP codes and counties.

    Sources and method

    Severe weather score. Expected yearly damage to an average home from tornadoes (40%), hail (40%) and strong wind (20%). FEMA National Risk Index, December 2025 release, census tracts weighted into ZIP codes and counties by 2020 Census housing units, then ranked across Oklahoma. 0 = lowest in the state, 100 = highest. Low 0-39, Moderate 40-69, High 70-89, Extreme 90-100. All of Oklahoma is above the U.S. average for hail and tornado damage.

    Wildfire score. Average of FEMA National Risk Index wildfire damage per home (December 2025) and USDA Forest Service Wildfire Risk to Communities risk to homes (2nd edition, 2024), each placed against all U.S. homes, then ranked within Oklahoma with the same tier ranges. These public measures approximate, but do not replicate, carrier scores such as Verisk FireLine.

    Recent wildfires. National Interagency Fire Center, WFIGS wildfire incident locations, October 7, 2025 to October 7, 2026. Reported incidents only; duplicate records removed.

    Tornado and hail history. NOAA Storm Prediction Center tornado database and NOAA NCEI Storm Events Database, 1990-2025.

    Homes and income. U.S. Census Bureau American Community Survey 2020-2024 5-year estimates. ZIP codes are Census ZIP Code Tabulation Areas. Gray areas inside the state on the map have no ZIP code (parks, military land, lakes). The full ZIP and county tables are in the accompanying workbook.
